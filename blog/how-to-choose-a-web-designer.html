How to Choose a Web Designer for Your Business

9 min read · Sohaib Younas

Hiring a web designer feels risky when you cannot code and cannot judge quality from a screenshot alone. Most business owners searching for a web designer end up comparing five or six quotes that all look similar on paper, then picking almost at random. This guide breaks the decision into checks you can actually run before you pay anyone, so you are choosing on evidence, not gut feeling.

Start With the Portfolio, Not the Pitch

Anyone can talk about "modern, responsive design." Ask instead for three live sites the web designer built, ideally in your industry or close to it. Open each one on your phone, not just your laptop, since most of your visitors will land on mobile first. Check load speed, check if text is readable without zooming, and check if buttons are easy to tap with a thumb. A portfolio full of nice screenshots but slow, broken live sites tells you the truth a sales pitch never will.

While you are looking at the portfolio, notice whether the designer's own website is well optimized. A web designer who cannot rank their own site on Google is unlikely to know how to rank yours either.

Ask What Happens After Launch

A website is not a one-time delivery. Domains renew, plugins need updates, and content changes. Before hiring, ask directly: who owns the code, who hosts it, and what happens if you want a small text change six months later. A web designer who has a clear answer, even if it costs extra, is more trustworthy than one who avoids the question. This single conversation prevents most of the disputes that come up later in freelance web design projects.

Check for SEO Awareness, Not Just Visuals

A beautiful site that never shows up on Google is not doing its job. Ask if the web designer sets up basic on-page SEO by default: page titles, meta descriptions, image alt text, and a sitemap submitted to Google Search Console. Some web designers are also SEO specialists, and that combination is genuinely valuable, since a site built with search visibility in mind from day one performs very differently from one where SEO is bolted on afterward. If the answer is "SEO is a separate service," that is fine, just budget for it separately instead of assuming it is included in the base price.

Web Designer vs. Digital Marketing Agency: Know the Difference

This is a distinction a lot of business owners miss. A web designer builds and designs your site. A digital marketing agency runs ongoing campaigns, ads, social media, and content strategy around that site. Some freelancers and small studios do both, but many specialize in one or the other. If your goal is simply a professional, fast, well-structured website, a dedicated web designer is usually the more cost-effective choice. If you already have a site and need consistent traffic, leads, and brand visibility over time, you are looking for digital marketing help, and the conversation, timeline, and pricing model look completely different. Knowing which one you actually need before you start requesting quotes saves you from comparing apples to oranges.

Freelance Web Designer or an Agency?

A freelance web designer usually costs less than an agency for the same scope of work, because there is no overhead of account managers or a larger team layer. You also get to talk directly to the person actually building your site, which tends to be faster and more direct. The tradeoff is that a single freelancer has less redundancy: if they get busy or unavailable, there is no backup team member to step in. For a single business website, most owners find a skilled, well-reviewed freelance web designer delivers better value than an agency, provided the freelancer has a clear process, a written scope, and real client feedback to show for it.

If You're Selling Products, Ask About eCommerce Experience Specifically

Not every web designer who builds business websites also knows how to build a store. If you plan to sell online, ask specifically whether they have experience as a WooCommerce developer or with Shopify, since eCommerce sites involve payment gateway setup, product structuring, tax rules, and inventory considerations that a brochure-style website never touches. A web designer with genuine WooCommerce developer experience will be able to talk fluently about product variations, shipping zones, and checkout optimization. If they hesitate on these topics, that is a sign eCommerce is not really their specialty, even if they are excellent at general web design.

Compare Pricing on Scope, Not Just the Number

A 150 dollar quote and a 1,500 dollar quote are not comparable unless you know what each includes. Get a written scope: number of pages, number of revisions, whether the price includes hosting setup, and whether stock images or copywriting are included. Cheap quotes that skip these details often cost more later in change requests, because every "small addition" gets billed separately once the base scope is exhausted.

Red Flags Worth Walking Away From

  • No live examples of past work, only PDF mockups or unlaunched drafts.
  • Pressure to pay the full amount upfront before any contract or scope document exists.
  • Vague answers about who will own the final source files after payment.
  • No mention of mobile responsiveness unless you specifically ask first.
  • Every testimonial reads identically, which usually means they were written by the designer, not the client.

Questions Worth Asking on the First Call

  • "Can I see analytics or real results from a past project, not just the design itself?"
  • "What platform will my site be built on, and can I edit it myself later without hiring you again?"
  • "What's your process if I'm not happy with the first draft?"
  • "Do you handle basic SEO setup, or is that a separate service I should plan for?"

A web designer who answers these clearly and specifically, without getting defensive, is usually someone who has done this enough times to know exactly how the process should go. That confidence is worth more than any portfolio screenshot.

What a Genuine Discovery Call Feels Like

A strong first call with a web designer feels more like an interview of your business than a sales pitch. Expect questions about who your customers are, what your competitors' sites look like, what result you actually want (more calls, more sales, more credibility), and what has not worked for you before if this is not your first website. If the entire call is spent talking about the designer's own process and portfolio with almost no questions asked about your business, that is a sign the eventual site may end up generic rather than built around what your specific customers respond to.

Spotting Real Reviews vs. Written-to-Order Testimonials

Testimonials are easy to fake and hard to verify, but a few checks help. Genuine reviews usually mention specific, verifiable details, a real project name, a specific result, a timeframe, rather than generic praise like "amazing to work with, highly recommend." Where possible, ask for a reference you can actually contact directly, not just a screenshot of a message. A web designer confident in their own work will rarely hesitate to connect you with a past client for a two-minute conversation.

What "Ownership" Actually Means in Practice

When a web designer says you will "own" your website, get specific about what that covers. Does it include the domain name registration, or is that registered under the designer's account? Is the hosting account in your name and payment method, or theirs? Do you receive the actual source files, or only access through a locked platform you cannot export from easily? These details rarely matter until the relationship ends, at which point they matter enormously. Settling this in writing before the project starts avoids a very common and very frustrating dispute later.

The Short Version

Check real live sites on mobile, get a written scope, ask about ownership and maintenance, confirm whether SEO is included, and clarify eCommerce experience if you are selling products. Whether you end up hiring a solo freelance web designer or a full digital marketing agency, the businesses that get the best results are the ones that ask these questions before signing anything, not after.
